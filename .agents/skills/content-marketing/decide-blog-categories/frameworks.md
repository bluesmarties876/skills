# Frameworks behind decide-blog-categories

Not loaded by default. Each section gives the rule the method borrows, the step that uses
it, and the primary source. The method in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) is complete without this
file; read it to justify a recommendation or to answer "where does that come from?".

## Content mission and content tilt (Joe Pulizzi)

**Rule.** A content mission has three parts: the specific audience, what you will deliver,
and the outcome for the audience. The tilt is the angle inside that audience where you
face little competition. A topic that fails the mission is out of scope, whatever its
search volume.

**Used in** step 2, the fence.

**Sources.** Joe Pulizzi, *Epic Content Marketing* (McGraw-Hill, 2013), summarized by
Content Marketing Institute in
[2 Essential Elements for Getting Started With Content Marketing](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/articles/essentials-getting-started-content-marketing/);
the three questions and the tilt are restated at
[The Tilt](https://www.thetilt.com/audience/content-mission-statement-explained).

## Core content strategy (Kristina Halvorson and Melissa Rach)

**Rule.** One sentence sets the long-term direction: what the content must achieve, what
content product you will make, and what the organization must do to support it. Content
earns its place by supporting a business objective or meeting a user need.

**Used in** step 2, as the test that the mission serves the business as well as the
reader.

**Source.** Kristina Halvorson and Melissa Rach, *Content Strategy for the Web*, 2nd ed.
(New Riders, 2012),
[chapter 7, "Core"](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/content-strategy-for/9780132883269/ch07.html).

## Jobs to Be Done (Clayton Christensen)

**Rule.** People hire a product, or an article, to make progress in a specific situation.
The job, not the customer profile or the product line, is the unit of analysis. Write it as
"When I [situation], I want to [progress], so I can [outcome]."

**Used in** step 3, and in step 7's rejection of department and product-line categories.

**Sources.** Clayton M. Christensen, Scott D. Anthony, Gerald Berstell and Denise
Nitterhouse,
[Finding the Right Job for Your Product](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/finding-the-right-job-for-your-product/),
*MIT Sloan Management Review* (2007); Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon and
David S. Duncan,
[Know Your Customers' "Jobs to Be Done"](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done),
*Harvard Business Review* (September 2016).

## They Ask, You Answer: The Big 5 (Marcus Sheridan)

**Rule.** Answer the questions buyers actually ask, including the uncomfortable ones. Five
subject types drive the most qualified traffic: cost and price, problems, comparisons,
reviews, and best-of lists.

**Used in** step 1 (the sales and support question) and step 3.

**Source.** Marcus Sheridan, *They Ask, You Answer*, 2nd ed. (Wiley, 2019),
[chapter 9, "The Discovery of The Big 5"](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/they-ask-you/9781119610144/c09.xhtml).

## See, Think, Do, Care (Avinash Kaushik)

**Rule.** Split the largest addressable qualified audience by intent: See (no commercial
intent), Think (weak intent), Do (ready to act), Care (existing customers). Content must
exist for all four.

**Used in** steps 4 and 5.

**Sources.** Avinash Kaushik,
[See-Think-Do: A Content, Marketing, Measurement Business Framework](https://www.kaushik.net/avinash/see-think-do-content-marketing-measurement-business-framework/)
and
[See, Think, Do, Care Winning Combo](https://www.kaushik.net/avinash/see-think-do-care-win-content-marketing-measurement/).

## Consumer decision journey (McKinsey)

**Rule.** Purchase decisions loop through initial consideration, active evaluation,
closure and postpurchase. Active evaluation is dominated by consumer-driven touchpoints
such as search and reviews, and postpurchase is the phase companies most under-serve.

**Used in** step 5, as the reason Think and Care gaps matter.

**Source.** David Court, Dave Elzinga, Susan Mulder and Ole Jørgen Vetvik,
[The consumer decision journey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-consumer-decision-journey),
*McKinsey Quarterly*, June 2009.

## Search intent (Andrei Broder)

**Rule.** Queries are navigational (reach a known site), informational (learn something)
or transactional (do something on the web).

**Used in** step 4: informational and early transactional demand belongs on the blog;
navigational demand belongs on the page it names.

**Source.** Andrei Broder,
[A taxonomy of web search](https://sigir.org/files/forum/F2002/broder.pdf),
*SIGIR Forum* 36, no. 2 (2002): 3–10.

## Topic clusters (HubSpot)

**Rule.** A topic is a goal, problem or idea the customer cares about. Each topic has one
broad pillar page and cluster posts on subtopics, which link back to the pillar.

**Used in** step 6: each category is a pillar with three to six subtopics.

**Sources.** HubSpot,
[Topics, pillar pages, and subtopic keywords](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/content-strategy/pillar-pages-topics-and-subtopics)
and
[How We Used the Pillar-Cluster Model to Transform Our Blog](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/pillar-cluster-model-transform-blog).

## Hero, Hub, Help (Google)

**Rule.** Help is always-on content people search for; Hub is regular programming around
the audience's passions; Hero is a rare, broad moment. Most output is Help.

**Used in** step 7, as the intended balance inside the categories. It does not choose
subjects.

**Sources.** Google,
[YouTube Playbook](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/1601/youtube-playbook.pdf)
and
[Constantly Connected Consumer Guidebook](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/1090/Google_AlwaysOn_Guide.pdf),
Think with Google.

## User needs (BBC World Service, Dmitry Shishkin)

**Rule.** People read for different needs: update me, educate me, give me perspective,
inspire me, divert me, keep me on trend; version 2.0 adds help me and connect me. In the
BBC rollout, one site produced about 70% of its output as "update me" and drew about 7% of
its traffic from it.

**Used in** step 7's news-only check.

**Sources.** Dmitry Shishkin, interviewed in
[5 questions about user needs](https://smartocto.com/blog/5-questions-about-user-needs/);
[User Needs Model 2.0](https://smartocto.com/user-needs/), smartocto.

## Card sorting and tree testing (Nielsen Norman Group)

**Rule.** In an open card sort, readers group topics and name the groups, which reveals
labels that match their mental model. A tree test then checks whether people can find a
known item in the proposed structure. Card sorting suggests groups; it does not hand over
the final menu.

**Used in** the recommended next step.

**Sources.** Samhita Tankala and Katie Sherwin,
[Card Sorting: Uncover Users' Mental Models](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/card-sorting-definition/),
Nielsen Norman Group, 2 February 2024;
[Tree Testing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tree-testing/), Nielsen Norman Group.
